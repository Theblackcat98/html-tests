The iceremembers

Every bubble in this shelf is a sealed sample of the air that was there when the snow fell. Scroll, and the section opens downward: firn, close-off, blue ice, the crevasse wall where the light gives out. Nothing moves. That is the point of the place.

Field log, 14 March — surface instruments, closed

214 m

Recovery

Core B-17 came up in three runs. The bottom metre was wet, refrozen, and not counted. Everything above it is archived, logged, and colder than it has any right to be in a liquid-water summer.

−38 °C

Basal temperature

Cold enough that the shelf is not melting at the bed, only flexing. The tide below flexes it. Twice a day the whole 40-kilometre slab lifts by eleven centimetres and settles again.

1 100 yr

Oldest sealed air

At 214 metres the gas is about eleven centuries younger than the ice holding it. The mismatch is not an error. It is the difference between how long it takes snow to fall and how long it takes a bubble to stop moving.

0 dB

Acoustic record

No wind, no traffic, no meltwater. A station on the shelf produces one sound every few minutes and it is the winch. Everything else in the log is silence, measured and published.
