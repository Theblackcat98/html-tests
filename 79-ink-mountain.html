空山不語

Kōzan fu go — empty mountains, without speech
空山不語　雪落無聲 孤舟一縷　燈如豆明 松下問客　山不應我 我亦不語　但見白雲 漸滿空江 Empty mountains do not speak. Snow falls without sound.
One thread of boat, a lamp bright as a bean.
I ask a guest beneath the pines; the mountain does not answer me.
I do not answer either — only white cloud, filling the empty river.

Six ridges, and the painter is careful never to draw the sixth completely. The emptiness is the subject; the black is only what is left when you take the mist away. Every wash here is one colour, laid once, and never worked back.

The lamp on the boat is the only warm mark on the silk. It is two millimetres across and it is the reason the whole composition is read from the bottom up.

At the left of the slider the ridges are a dilute grey wash, five minutes old. At the right the near cliff is standing black, laid in one stroke and left to dry on the mounting. Nothing in between is allowed to sit.

松下問客 — notes on what the painter left out

六

Six ridges, four drawn

The sixth is below the frame. In this manner two ridges are painted fully, two are cut by the mist, and the last is implied by the shape of the mist itself. A viewer who has been taught anything will supply the rest, and will believe they saw it.

留白

Leaving white

About sixty per cent of the silk is untouched. This is not modesty or lack of time. The unpainted field is water, air and distance, and it is the only place the eye can rest, so the painter spends the ink everywhere else.

一舟

One boat, one lamp

A single boat gives the river a scale. Two would give it a fleet, and a fleet is a story. The lantern is the only pigment warmer than grey in the whole leaf, laid last, over dry silk, in one touch.

四

Four birds, not nine

Birds are counted by the painter's breath, not by the eye. Four strokes are enough to place the air between them and the ridge. More would start a conversation about the birds themselves.
