Wood remembers
which way the tree grew.

A cabinet front is not a picture. It is four veneers, a boxwood line and a decision about direction. In this one the walnut is book-matched about the centre stile, the field is cross-banded so the light changes as you walk past it, and the rosette is eight separate pieces cut from a single packet — no two rays are the same piece of wood, which is exactly the point.

The finish

Eleven coats of shellac, cut back between each one, then two of matt varnish and a hand-rubbed wax. Nothing is sprayed. The sheen you can move across the front is not a texture — it is the coat, at 62° to the light.

One drawer is left plain on purpose. After two panels of figuration, an unfigured field reads louder than either of them.

Bench controls

Ninety years of handling take a piece from matte to mirror. Move the polish up and the specular band sharpens; at zero the front goes dry and flat, the way a freshly scraped panel does. Hover an inlay to read its packet.
