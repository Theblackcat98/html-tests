Type has no shadow. Until now.

TYPE
HAS NO
SHADOW.
UNTIL NOW.

A letter on a screen is a claim about depth that it has not earned. For four centuries we faked it with hatching, then with a bevel, then with a soft grey smudge. This issue argues for the blunt one — the slab, the zero blur, the shadow that lands exactly where you told it to and refuses to soften. Move the pointer. The three duplicate plates swap. Nothing is blending; everything is offset. That is the whole trick, and it has never been more literal.

01 / CRAFT

The bevel is a confession

Every gradient edge on a letter is an admission that the designer could not decide where the surface ended. A hard shadow decides for you, in the first frame, with a number.

6 min · Ridgway
02 / ARCHIVE

Misregistration as an error, then as a style

Two-plate lithography could not hold a register and the pressman either cursed or leaned in. The cyan and magenta drifts of 1970s posters are the same accident, repeated on purpose.

9 min · Okonkwo
03 / THEORY

Three plates and no gradient

A word carries one mark and two memories of where it was. Depth is not light, it is arithmetic: displacement multiplied, then not softened. Physics, minus the atmosphere.

4 min · Vollenweider
04 / PRINT

Ink spreads, screens do not

Halftone dots bloom on damp stock. The screen has no such excuse, which is why screen designers over-invent. Blur is a habit, not a light source.

5 min · Ferreira
