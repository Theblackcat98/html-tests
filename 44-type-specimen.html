03 Waterfall
04 Text

Meridian began as a correction. The brief was a display grotesque that could survive being set at eleven pixels on a departures board, which is a harder problem than it sounds: at that size a wide bowl becomes a blot, a tight aperture becomes a hole, and anything with a personality starts shouting over its neighbours. So the counters were opened first, the joins flattened, the terminals cut on a single angle, and only then was anybody allowed to have fun with the tail on the q.

What came back is a face that changes its mind with the size. The optical size axis is not a nicety bolted on at the end; it is the reason the 8 pt setting is a different drawing from the 72 pt setting. Turn the optical slider down and watch the spacing tighten, the apertures narrow and the counters plump out. Turn the weight up and the joins fill first, the way a punch does. The width axis is the strangest of the four, and the one we get asked about least: stretch it past 120 and the face stops being a grotesque and starts being a banner.

It is a workhorse that has opinions. Set it in a single weight and it behaves. Set a whole paragraph at 400 and it will read like a colleague who has read the memo. The italic is a true italic, drawn with a forward angle of eleven degrees and a single-storey a, because a two-storey a at small sizes is a staircase in a bad mood.
